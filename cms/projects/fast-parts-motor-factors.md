## At a glance

- **Client:** Fast Parts Motor Factors, Northwich and Winsford, Cheshire (serving the area since 1989)
- **What they do:** aftermarket parts for most cars and vans, plus tools and consumables, for trade customers and the public
- **What we did:** a new website, built, hosted and launched by Pixel North
- **Live site:** fastparts.uk.com (launched 5 Nov 2026)
- **Tagline:** "The right parts, fast."

## The business

Fast Parts is a small independent motor factor with two shops, one in Northwich and one in Winsford. Garages rely on them for quick trade orders, and local drivers come in or call for the right part for their own car or van. Speed and knowing the job are what customers value, and the reviews say so: the site shows a 4.8 out of 5 Google rating from ten five-star reviews (checked 26 Oct 2026), with customers praising helpful staff, good knowledge and quick delivery.

## The challenge

The old website didn't reflect the business. Specifically:

- It didn't make the two audiences clear: a garage wanting a trade delivery and a driver wanting one part are different visitors with different questions.
- Some of the copy was wrong. It talked about "new and recon parts", but Fast Parts doesn't sell reconditioned parts.
- There was no easy way to ask for a part online and have it reach the right shop.
- Nothing about it said "local, family-run, been here since 1989".

## The approach

**Start with the real business.** We kept Fast Parts' own logo (recreated at a higher resolution where only a small version existed) and used genuine photos of the team, the van and the shop counter, rather than stock images. We began with mock-ups of the home page and the Trade page, so Paul could react to something real before any build work.

**Say it plainly.** The headline is "The right parts, fast." The home page opens with what they sell and who for, then points trade and retail visitors to their own pages. Facts were checked with Paul and corrected where the old site was wrong: Northwich and Winsford since 1989, no reconditioned parts, and a trade order cut-off of 09:45 for delivery by noon.

**Make it easy to act.**

- A **Send a Parts Enquiry** form asks for the vehicle registration, the part needed and the nearest shop. A visitor can optionally use their browser location to pick the nearest shop, and the site is clear that the location isn't received or stored.
- Both shops have their address, phone number, opening hours and a Google Maps directions link on a Find us page.
- A dedicated **Trade** page for garages, with the cut-off time and delivery promise up front. A trade credit-account section was removed entirely when it turned out not to fit how Fast Parts works, rather than being left in half-finished.

**Build it properly underneath.**

- Fast, simple pages that load quickly on a phone, hosted on Cloudflare, with a staging preview used to check every change before it went live.
- Clean web addresses and canonical tags throughout, a sitemap, and Google Search Console set up and verified on launch day so the site can be found.
- A branded error page, and a share preview image so links look right on WhatsApp and Facebook.
- Privacy done properly: a privacy notice, a cookie policy and a cookie banner where Reject is as easy as Accept. Google Analytics only loads after a visitor agrees, and each choice is recorded in a consent log.

## Launch

The move onto the real domain, fastparts.uk.com, happened on 5 Nov 2026. Paul owns the domain and his email runs on it, so the email records were carried across to the new DNS set-up first, so that his mailbox kept working throughout. The new site replaced the old one at the point the domain switched over, with nothing for visitors to do.

## What we learned

Fast Parts was the first full build to go through Pixel North's process from start to finish, and it shaped how every site since is built: the launch checklist, the shared cookie control, the standard setup (404 page, share image, clean addresses) and the habit of checking the live repo before every change. A good site starts with what the business actually does and says, and the details Paul corrected, such as no reconditioned parts and the 09:45 cut-off, did more for the site than any design choice.
